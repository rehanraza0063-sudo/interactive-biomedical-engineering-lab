Cardiac Arrhythmia
▪ Cardiac Arrhythmia is an irregularity of heartbeat that causes
 the heart to beat too slow or very fast.
▪ Some arrhythmias are dangerous and may require medical
 attention while some are less hazardous.
▪ An abnormally faster heart rate is called tachycardia.
▪ An abnormally slower heart rate is called bradycardia.

Causes of Arrhythmia
Causes of this arrhythmia
▪ Sinus node not able to generate enough heart beats.
▪ Rhythm of sinus node becomes abnormal.
▪ Atria takes over the functions of SA node.
▪ Interruption in the electrical wiring of the heart.
▪ Ventricles not able to beat effectively called ventricular fibrillation.

Symptoms
▪ Palpitations:
▪ Throbbing
▪ Pounding
▪ Thumping
▪ Fluttering
▪ Some people feel dizzy or pain
▪ Chest Pain

Types of Arrhythmia
▪ Arrhythmia may be classified by rate (normal, tachycardia,
 bradycardia), or mechanism (automaticity, reentry, fibrillation).
▪ It is also appropriate to classify by site of origin
  ▪ Atrial
  ▪ Ventricular

Atrial Arrhythmia
▪ Premature Atrial Contractions (PACs) are one type of premature
 heartbeat, irregular heartbeat, or benign arrhythmia. These ectopic
 beats occur in the upper two chambers of the heart, called the atria.
Causes:
▪ Some cells in the heart start to fire or go off before the normal
 heartbeat is supposed to occur. This happens in the sino atrial
 node (SA node). These can be cells that are sick, have been damaged
 by a virus

Atrial Arrhythmia
Wandering pacemaker
▪ In cardiology, a wandering pacemaker is an atrial arrhythmia that
 occurs when the natural cardiac pacemaker site shifts between the SA
 node, the atria, and/or the AV node.
Causes:
▪ A wandering pacemaker is usually caused by varying vagal tone.

Atrial Arrhythmia
▪ Atrial flutter (AFL) is an abnormal heart rhythm that occurs in
 the atria of the heart. When it first occurs, it is usually
 associated with a fast heart rate or tachycardia (beats over 100
 per minute)
▪ Causes: hypertension, coronary artery disease

Atrial Arrhythmia
▪ Atrial fibrillation (AF or A-fib) is the most common cardiac
 arrhythmia (abnormal heart rhythm), and involves the two upper
 chambers (atria) of the heart. Its name comes from the fibrillating (i.e.,
 quivering) of the heart muscles of the atria, instead of a coordinated
 contraction.
Causes: Hypertension (High blood pressure)
▪ Primary heart diseases including coronary artery disease, mitral
 stenosis
▪ Lung diseases
▪ Excessive alcohol consumption

Atrial Arrhythmia
▪ Supraventricular tachycardia (SVT) is any tachycardic rhythm
 originating above the ventricular tissue.

Atrial Arrhythmia
▪ Junctional    rhythm    describes    an    abnormal      heart
 rhythm resulting from impulses coming from a locus of tissue in
 the area of the atrioventricular node, the "junction" between
 atria and ventricles
▪ This can happen in the case of a block in conduction somewhere
 along     the     pathway.     When      this    happens,       the
 heart's atrioventricular node takes over as the pacemaker.

Ventricular Arrhythmia
▪ A Premature Ventricular Contraction       (PVC), is a relatively
 common event where the heartbeat is initiated by the
 heart ventricles rather than by the sinoatrial node.
▪ In a PVC, the ventricles contract first, which means that
 circulation is inefficient.

Ventricular Arrhythmia
▪ Ventricular fibrillation (V-fib or VF) is a condition in which there
  is uncoordinated contraction of the cardiac muscle of
  the ventricles in the heart, making them quiver rather than
  contract properly.
Causes:
▪ Abnormal automaticity
▪ Ventricular fibrillation is a cause of cardiac arrest and sudden
  cardiac death.

Arrhythmia Monitors
An arrhythmia monitor is basically a sophisticated alarm
system
▪ Scans ECG Patterns and issues alarms.
▪ Most significantly detects ventricular arrhythmias.
▪ With availability of low-cost PC’s desktop arrhythmia monitors
  are also available.
▪ Most are ventricular oriented.
▪ Detects premature QRS Complexes, widened QRS.
▪ Each patient's ECG may differ so, determinations are done on
  reference obtained from same patient.

Arrhythmia monitors operates in
following order
▪ Storing Normal QRS Complex, particularly QRS width and R-R
 Interval.
▪ Gives and alarm when any abnormal beat is detected.
▪ Gives alarm light signals whenever premature or widened
 varieties exist upto rate of 5/min or 12/min.
▪ Detects and Triggers alarms when artifacts are present at
 sources e.g. muscle tremor due to patient movement, base line
 shift and improperly connected electrodes.

Technique

Packard: Processes ECG for both paced and non paced patients

Actions performed
▪ Filtering signal
▪ Detecting and classifying QRS
▪ Generating heart rate
▪ Identifying irregular rhythms
▪ Generating alarms

P Wave and QRS Wave Detection
Technique

  ST/AR (ST and Arrhythmia) Algorithm by Hewlett
                     Packard

P Wave and QRS Wave Detection
Technique
                                          ▪ After a QRS complex is identified, a search is
                                            made on each lead independently in the area
                                            prior to the R wave to determine if there is
                                            an associated P wave.
                                          ▪ This area is 200 ms wide and ends 120 ms
                                            before the R wave peak.
                                          ▪ To be accepted as a P wave, it must be at
                                            least 1/32 of the R wave height and the P–R
                                            interval must be close to the average P–R
                                            interval.
                                          ▪ P wave detection is used to differentiate
                                            between     a   Sinus    Rhythm       and    a
                                            Supraventricular (SV) Rhythm.

P Wave and QRS Wave Detection
Technique
▪ Once the QRS is detected and measured, the beat is labelled.
▪ Labelling means that the algorithm assigns the complex one of the
  following labels:
   ▪ normal (N)
   ▪ supraventricular premature (S)
   ▪ ventricular ectopic (V)
   ▪ paced (P)
   ▪ questionable (?)
   ▪ learning (L).
   If the signal quality is not good, the algorithm assigns the label
   “inoperative (I)” and “artifact (A)”.

Exercise stress testing

  Stress    test   or     exercise
  electrocardiography is used
  when     the    diagnosis     of
  coronary arterial disease is
  suspected or to determine the
  physical           performance
  characteristics of a patient

 Exercise stress testing
                                                          HOW?
▪ The test involves
❖The recording of the electrocardiogram during dynamic or
 occasionally isometric exercise
❖Dynamic exercise is performed by the patient who walks on a
 treadmill on which the speed and elevation can be adjusted,
 manually or automatically, to suit a variety of graded exercise
 protocols
❖Alternatively, the patient may be asked to pedal an electrically
 braked bicycle ergometer.

Exercise stress testing                            TYPES?
▪ Two types of tests
❖Treadmill Test
❖Bicycle Test

Exercise stress testing
                                                        TYPES?
▪ Two types of tests
❖Treadmill Test
▪ The Balke-Ware Protocol: It uses a constant speed of 3.3
  miles/hour (5.3 km/hour), with progressive increments in the
  load every 2 minutes. This is achieved by increasing the grade or
  incline of the motor-driven treadmill. The exercising subject
  therefore walks “uphill” and his own body weight serves as the
  load.
▪ The Bruce Protocol: It uses simultaneous increments in both
  speed and treadmill grade at intervals of 3 minutes.

 Exercise stress testing
                                                               TYPES?
▪ Two types of tests
❖Bicycle Test
▪ In this test, the speed is usually kept constant and incremental
 resistance is applied either mechanically (friction belt or brake pads)
 or electrically.
▪ The work load can be precisely measured in terms of kilograms per
 metre per minute
▪ In bicycle ergometry, physiological measurements are more easily
 obtained with the subject comfortably seated and the torso relatively
 immobile
